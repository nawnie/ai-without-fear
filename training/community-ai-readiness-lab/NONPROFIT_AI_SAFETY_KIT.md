# Nonprofit AI Safety Kit

This kit provides starting templates for small organizations. It is educational material, not legal, privacy, cybersecurity, employment, financial, medical, or professional advice. Organizations should adapt it with qualified review where required.

---

# 1. One-Page Interim AI Policy Template

## Purpose

[Organization] may use approved artificial-intelligence tools to support low-risk drafting, organization, accessibility, brainstorming, and research tasks while protecting sensitive information and preserving accountable human judgment.

## Approved uses

Approved tools may be used for:

- drafting from already-approved public information
- brainstorming and outlining
- converting approved public material into alternate formats or reading levels
- organizing public research
- creating internal templates that contain no sensitive information
- other uses specifically approved by [role]

## Prohibited uses

AI tools may not be used to:

- enter passwords, credentials, access tokens, or recovery information
- upload confidential client, donor, employee, student, patient, legal, financial, or personnel information without explicit authorization and appropriate controls
- make final eligibility, disciplinary, employment, medical, legal, financial, safety, or other high-impact decisions
- impersonate a person or create deceptive media
- publish unverified factual claims or citations
- bypass existing approval, recordkeeping, consent, copyright, security, or professional requirements

## Data rule

- Green data may be used in approved tools.
- Yellow data requires an approved purpose, tool, and owner.
- Red data may not be entered unless the organization has explicitly authorized the workflow and confirmed appropriate legal, contractual, privacy, and security controls.

## Human review

A responsible human must review generated output for factual accuracy, source support, tone, accessibility, privacy, bias, and compliance before publication or action.

## Tool approval

[Role] maintains the approved-tool list and records the date each tool's data practices were reviewed.

## Disclosure

AI assistance will be disclosed when required by law, contract, funder rules, professional standards, organizational policy, or reasonable audience expectations.

## Incident reporting

Suspected exposure, harmful output, impersonation, unauthorized use, or policy violations must be reported immediately to [role/contact].

## Review date

This interim policy will be reviewed on [date] and at least every [frequency].

# 2. Data Traffic Light Card

## Green

Generally acceptable in approved tools:

- public website text
- approved event details
- public reports
- fictional examples
- sanitized templates
- properly licensed or public-domain material

## Yellow

Use only with approval:

- unpublished plans
- internal procedures
- draft budgets
- meeting notes
- vendor proposals
- non-public aggregate statistics

## Red

Do not enter without explicit authorization and appropriate controls:

- credentials and access tokens
- health information
- donor, client, employee, or student identities
- personnel records
- banking, tax, payment, or credit information
- legal case details
- precise locations of vulnerable people
- confidential investigations, contracts, or complaints

## Rule

Uncertain means yellow or red until reviewed.

# 3. Human Review Checklist

Before using or publishing output:

- [ ] names are correct
- [ ] dates and times match the source
- [ ] numbers were checked
- [ ] quotations appear in the original
- [ ] links open
- [ ] citations support the claim
- [ ] no confidential information is present
- [ ] no unsupported promise, benefit, or urgency was added
- [ ] tone and reading level fit the audience
- [ ] accessibility requirements were considered
- [ ] required professional or leadership review occurred
- [ ] disclosure requirements were followed

# 7. Ninety-Day Adoption Plan

## Days 1-30

- adopt interim policy
- inventory known tools
- stop or remediate red-data uses
- name tool owner and incident contact
- test one green-data workflow

## Days 31-60

- review top three tools
- train remaining staff and volunteers
- document approved prompts or procedures
- collect workflow quality and correction data

## Days 61-90

- review incidents and near misses
- revise policy
- decide whether to expand, pause, or retire the pilot workflow
- brief the board or leadership
- set six-month goals
