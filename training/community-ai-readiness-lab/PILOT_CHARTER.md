# Pilot Charter - Fearless First 10

## 1. Pilot purpose

The Fearless First 10 pilot will help ten western North Carolina organizations adopt artificial intelligence with practical guardrails. The pilot is designed to produce visible, measurable changes in organizational practice rather than a stack of certificates slowly becoming one with a filing cabinet.

## 2. Intended participants

Priority participants include:

- small nonprofits
- public libraries
- community and workforce organizations
- arts and cultural organizations
- faith-based service programs
- community colleges
- rural service organizations
- tribal or Indigenous partners where the work is invited and collaboratively designed

Each organization should send one or two people who can influence daily workflow or policy. Executive directors, program managers, office administrators, communications staff, librarians, volunteer coordinators, and board members are all suitable.

## 3. Pilot scope

The pilot includes:

- six 90-minute facilitated sessions
- a learner workbook
- the Nonprofit AI Safety Kit
- office-hour support between sessions
- a pre-assessment and post-assessment
- a 30-day follow-up
- a 90-day follow-up
- an anonymized public impact brief

The pilot does **not** include:

- legal advice
- cybersecurity certification
- vendor endorsement
- automated processing of client, patient, donor, employee, student, financial, legal, or other sensitive data
- custom software development for participating organizations
- permission to paste real confidential information into public AI tools

## 4. Required outputs

Each organization leaves with:

1. a one-page interim AI policy
2. a Data Traffic Light classification sheet
3. an inventory of AI tools already in use
4. a human-review checklist
5. one documented low-risk workflow
6. a 30-day adoption or remediation plan
7. a short board or leadership briefing

## 5. Learning outcomes

By the end of the pilot, participants should be able to:

- explain generative AI without calling it magic, sentience, or a search engine with better manners
- identify common failure patterns, including fabricated facts, unsupported citations, bias, privacy leakage, and automation bias
- decide which information is safe to place into an approved tool
- write a task-specific prompt with context, constraints, and an output format
- verify names, dates, numbers, quotations, sources, and high-impact claims
- compare AI tools using a consistent review worksheet
- draft organizational rules for approved, restricted, and prohibited uses
- document one workflow that includes human review and a fallback path

## 6. Safety rules

During training:

- use fictional, public, or deliberately sanitized examples only
- never paste sensitive production data into a demonstration tool
- never upload participant lists, donor records, health information, legal files, personnel records, financial records, credentials, or unpublished client information
- use demo accounts owned by the training program when possible
- require human review before any generated material is published or acted upon
- stop the exercise if a participant accidentally exposes sensitive information
- record the event in the training incident log and follow the host organization's response procedure

## 7. Pilot metrics

| Measure | Target |
|---|---:|
| Participating organizations | 10 |
| Individual participants | 20-30 |
| Organizations completing a tool inventory | 10 |
| Organizations drafting an interim policy | 8 or more |
| Documented low-risk workflows | 10 |
| Participants completing pre/post assessment | 80% or more |
| Host or referral partners | 3 |
| Sensitive-data incidents during training | 0 |
| Public impact brief | 1 |

## 8. Evidence collection

Collect:

- attendance by session
- pre/post knowledge scores
- scenario-based judgment scores
- policy completion status
- workflow documentation rubric scores
- participant confidence, separated from participant accuracy
- 30-day implementation status
- 90-day implementation status
- short interviews and partner testimonials

Confidence and competence are not the same metric. The program should measure both so we do not accidentally manufacture very enthusiastic errors.

## 9. Staffing model

Minimum staffing:

- one lead facilitator
- one technical or classroom support person
- one program administrator or partner contact

Recommended advisory support:

- nonprofit operations
- privacy or information governance
- adult education
- accessibility
- local community representation

## 10. Partner requirements

A host partner should provide or help arrange:

- accessible meeting space or online platform
- participant recruitment
- internet access and display equipment
- a point of contact
- reasonable accommodations
- permission to collect anonymized evaluation data

## 11. Pilot calendar

| 1 | Session 1 - AI Without the Fog Machine |

| 7 | Completion support and partner debrief |
| 10 | 30-day follow-up |
| 18 | 90-day follow-up and impact brief |

## 12. Go/no-go gates

Launch only when the program has:

- at least three independent board directors or a documented fiscal-sponsor governance path
- a host partner
- at least six committed organizations, with ten preferred
- a published privacy notice for evaluation data
- a facilitator and backup facilitator
- approved demo tools and sanitized exercises
- an incident-response procedure
- clear ownership of curriculum and participant materials
